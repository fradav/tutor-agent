16  References

Readings to understand the pedagogical framework and the technique

16.1 Why this design: theoretical framework

The references below ground the module’s pedagogical design (see teaching-design.qmd and ai-tutor.qmd). They are given to help you situate the course; none needs to be read fully to succeed, but each can inform a part of your reflexive report.

  • Co-constructed learning: knowledge is built in interaction [1], and students take part in designing their own track [2]–[4], in the tradition of critical pedagogy [5].
  • Learning contracts: how to write objectives, sequencing and success criteria; the Waterloo Centre for Teaching Excellence factsheet [6].
  • Learning by teaching with AI: teaching an agent structures learning [7]; conversational tutoring best practices preserve the student’s agency and integrate knowledge tracing [8]; a randomized trial (N=194) suggests that well-designed AI tutoring can outperform active learning in class [9]; a result to be confirmed.
  • Dialogue and reflexivity: dialogic pedagogy applied to LLMs [10].

16.2 Documented limits of AI in education

The module treats these limits as objects of work, not taboos:

  • Hallucinations and reinforcement of misconceptions [11].
  • Over-reliance and risk of losing basic skills [12]; cognitive offloading and “cognitive debt” [13], [14]; instrumental trust in an assistant [15].
  • Often superficial student uses [16] and adoption conditioned by trust and institutional policy [17], [18].
  • Resistance, access inequalities and postures toward technology [19], [20]; student viewpoints [21]; state of the art of pedagogical agents [22].
  • For those interested in technique as individuation and not merely a tool: [23].

16.3 Official technical resources

The primary reference for the technique remains the official documentation; your tutor (Track A) and your own research (Track B) must always be checkable against it.

  • Asynchronous Python: asyncio [24].
  • Multiprocess and IPC: multiprocessing [25].
  • Distributed computing: Dask delayed [26] and Ray [27].
  • Vectorization: numpy ufunc [28] and numba [29].
  • GPU: CuPy [30] and numba CUDA [29].
  • WebGPU: WebGPU specification [31] and the WGSL language [32].
